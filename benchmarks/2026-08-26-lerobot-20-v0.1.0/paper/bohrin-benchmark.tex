\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Static analyzers for robot demonstration data promise to catch defects that degrade a
behavior-cloned policy before training begins. Their detectors are typically validated by
fault injection: plant a known defect in a fixture, confirm the detector fires. That
measures recall and cannot measure precision, because a detector that fires unconditionally
passes every such test. We report the complementary measurement for \emph{bohrin}, an
open-source dataset linter: how often its 46 default detectors complain about data that
other people collected, curated, published, and trained on. Scanning 20 public LeRobot
datasets (4{,}342 episodes, 545{,}964 frames, 2--40 action dimensions, 5--50\,Hz, simulated
and real), all 20 parsed without error in 22.3\,s on a laptop CPU, yielding 189 findings
(23 \textsc{high}, 121 \textsc{medium}, 45 \textsc{low}). Twenty-seven detectors fired at
least once, nineteen never fired, and four ever reached \textsc{high}. One detector,
\dtr{dynamics.inverse\_residual}, reports \textsc{high} on 50\% of the corpus; we give
three competing explanations, two of which are defects in the tool, and identify an
ill-conditioned ridge solve as the first hypothesis to test. We also find that the default
300-episode triage cap changes no \textsc{high} finding and two \textsc{medium} findings
out of 189. We state plainly what this measurement does not establish: without ground-truth
labels a fire rate is a rate of complaint, not a rate of correctness, and no policy was
trained, so the premise that these defects degrade policies remains untested here. A
secondary finding concerns the ecosystem rather than the tool: 18 of 20 datasets declare
\texttt{robot\_type: "unknown"}, undermining any embodiment-keyed calibration built on
public metadata.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}

A data-quality detector encodes a claim: that a specific pattern in the data predicts a
specific training failure. Testing that claim by fault injection establishes recall and
nothing else. A detector hard-coded to return \textsc{high} passes every such test.

Precision determines whether such a tool is usable in practice. A \textsc{high} severity
that fires on most curated public datasets carries no information, and it breaks a
\texttt{-{}-fail-on HIGH} continuous-integration gate for every user. Measuring precision
requires real data and a count of complaints. This report is that measurement.

\section{Corpus and Method}

Twenty LeRobot datasets were selected for breadth of embodiment and provenance rather than
size. Fourteen are Open X-Embodiment datasets \cite{oxe} that reached LeRobot through an
RLDS conversion; six were published natively for LeRobot. Four are simulated. Action
dimensionality spans 2 to 40 and control rates span 5\,Hz to 50\,Hz. Bohrin does not decode
video, so the corpus occupies roughly 50\,MB.

Embodiments were confirmed from published sources where possible: \dtr{asu\_table\_top} is
a UR5 commanding joint velocities, \dtr{austin\_buds\_dataset} and
\dtr{nyu\_franka\_play\_dataset} are Franka arms \cite{tfds},
\dtr{cmu\_stretch} is a Hello Robot Stretch \cite{stretch}, \dtr{dlr\_edan\_shared\_control}
is a wheelchair-mounted 8-DoF DLR arm \cite{edan}, and \dtr{utokyo\_saytap} is a Unitree Go1
quadruped \cite{saytap}, the one locomotion dataset in an otherwise manipulation-heavy
corpus. Platforms not verified against a primary source are not claimed.

Each dataset was scanned twice. The \textbf{full} pass reads every episode and is the basis
for every number reported here. The \textbf{default} pass is what a user gets from
\texttt{bohrin scan}: a triage scan capped at 300 episodes, which subsamples 3 of the 20
datasets. Section~\ref{sec:triage} compares them. Both passes used no policy checkpoint, no
target family, no calibration corpus, and no vision.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Coverage and cost}

All 20 datasets parsed, profiled, and reported with no crash and no adapter failure. Total
wall-clock time was 22.3\,s on a laptop CPU with no GPU, median 0.8\,s per dataset.
Seventeen of twenty produced at least one \textsc{high}; none came back clean.

Wall time correlates with episode count at $r = 0.87$ and with frame count at only
$r = 0.68$ (Figure~\ref{fig:runtime}), so per-episode fixed work dominates rather than raw
data volume: \dtr{aloha\_mobile\_cabinet} reads 127{,}500 frames in 2.1\,s while
\dtr{ucsd\_pick\_and\_place} reads about half that in 4.2\,s with $16\times$ more episodes.
Growth is sub-linear, with $97\times$ more episodes costing $10.5\times$ more time.

\subsection{\dtr{dynamics.inverse\_residual} fails its own audit}
\label{sec:suspect}

The detector fires on 19 of 20 datasets and returns \textsc{high} on 10 of 20. A
\textsc{high} on half of a curated, published, widely used corpus is more likely a threshold
defect than a real epidemic. Three explanations are consistent with the number, and this
run cannot fully separate them.

\textbf{The ridge solve is ill-conditioned.} These scans emitted
\dtr{LinAlgWarning: Ill-conditioned matrix}
($\mathrm{rcond} = 7.05\times10^{-17}$) from \dtr{sklearn.linear\_model.\_ridge}, alongside divide-by-zero, overflow, and invalid-value
warnings from \dtr{sklearn.utils.extmath}. The detector fits an inverse-dynamics model
$g(o_t, o_{t+1}) \rightarrow \hat{a}_t$ and thresholds its residual; if the design matrix is
near-singular the fit is unreliable and so is the residual. This is a concrete, checkable
hypothesis and the first thing to test.

\textbf{The threshold ignores control rate.} Fourteen of twenty datasets run at 5\,Hz. At
200\,ms per step, consecutive states are only weakly related by the action between them, so
an inverse-dynamics residual is legitimately large without any misalignment. A threshold
calibrated on higher-rate data would over-fire here systematically.

\textbf{The finding is real.} Action/observation misalignment is a known hazard of format
conversion, and 14 of the 20 datasets reached LeRobot through an RLDS conversion of an
already-converted OXE dataset. Two conversion hops is a plausible place to lose
synchronization.

The first two explanations are defects in the tool; the third is a defect in the data.
Separating them requires the adjudication described in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\subsection{The nineteen silent detectors}

Nineteen of the 46 default detectors produced no finding across 4{,}342 episodes. This is
not evidence they are broken: several target defects this corpus should not have
(\dtr{integrity.nan\_inf}), the vision family had decoding disabled, and the entire
\textsc{policy}$\leftrightarrow$\textsc{data} family stays silent without a checkpoint. It
does mean those detectors carry no real-data evidence in this run, and their default
severities rest on synthetic fixtures alone. It would be incorrect to describe this run as
having validated 46 detectors.

\subsection{Triage sampling does not change the conclusions}
\label{sec:triage}

The default scan caps at 300 episodes, subsampling \dtr{ucsd\_pick\_and\_place} (300 of
1{,}355), \dtr{xarm\_lift\_medium} (300 of 800), and \dtr{nyu\_franka\_play} (300 of 456).
It reads 61\% of episodes and 85\% of frames in 21.5\,s against 22.3\,s.

Every \textsc{high} finding is identical between the two passes, as are the counts of
datasets scanned, datasets with at least one \textsc{high}, detectors that fired, and
detectors reaching \textsc{high}. Two \textsc{medium} findings out of 189 differ
(119 versus 121), and four detectors shift by one dataset each, all on the three subsampled
datasets. Triage is a reasonable default on this corpus; that is a statement about these 20
datasets, not a general guarantee.

\section{Two Detectors Were Correctly Held Back}
\label{sec:excluded}

Bohrin's registry defines \texttt{DEFAULT\_EXCLUDED}: detectors that are implemented and
tested but withheld from a default scan pending recalibration, reachable with
\texttt{-{}-all}. It holds \dtr{smoothness.discontinuity\_jump} and
\dtr{integrity.declared\_mismatch}, excluded after an earlier run of this harness measured
them reporting \textsc{high} on 70\% and 60\% of the corpus. Neither appears in any finding
here, confirming the mechanism works. This is the project's alternative to deleting a noisy
detector: measure it, record the number, and gate it.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

These bound every number above.

\textbf{Fire rate is not precision.} Nothing here establishes that a single finding is
correct. A 95\% fire rate is equally consistent with ``95\% of public robot datasets have
this defect'' and with ``the threshold is too tight.'' No ground-truth labels exist for this
corpus and none were constructed. Every rate reported here is a rate of complaint. The next
step is to hand-adjudicate a stratified sample and publish a precision estimate with an
explicit $N$.

\textbf{No link to downstream policy performance.} This is the most important gap. The
premise behind every detector is that the defect it finds degrades a trained policy. This
report does not test that premise: no policy was trained, no defect was ablated, no success
rate was measured. The mechanisms are drawn from the literature, not from an experiment run
here. A defensible causal claim requires training policies on data with and without a given
defect and measuring the difference in rollout success. Until then, severity is a triage
ordering rather than a prediction of harm.

\textbf{Selection bias, in both directions.} The datasets were chosen by the project's own
maintainer. They are curated and widely used, which biases toward clean data and makes a
high fire rate more suspicious, the intended direction. They are also predominantly 5\,Hz
OXE conversions, so conversion artifacts are over-represented and native high-rate
teleoperation is under-represented.

\textbf{Single machine, single run.} One laptop CPU with no GPU, one pass per configuration,
warm cache. Timings are indicative rather than benchmarked: no repeats, no confidence
intervals.

\textbf{Default configuration only.} No checkpoint, target, calibration corpus, or vision.
The five \textsc{policy}$\leftrightarrow$\textsc{data} detectors were inert by construction.

\textbf{The conformal gate never activated, and public metadata may prevent fixing that.}
Bohrin's \texttt{-{}-fpr} becomes a false-discovery bound only when a calibration corpus
covers the dataset's embodiment, using embodiment as a Mondrian category \cite{vovk}. No
corpus ships with the package, so every scan fell back to a robust-$z$ heuristic. Filling
that gap meets an obstacle this run surfaced incidentally: \textbf{18 of the 20 datasets
declare} \mbox{\texttt{robot\_type:\,"unknown"}}, with only the two ALOHA datasets naming an
embodiment. A Mondrian taxonomy keyed on a field that 90\% of this corpus leaves blank
collapses to a single wildcard bucket and forfeits the group-conditional validity the design
was chosen for.

\textbf{Numerical warnings were observed and not resolved.} An ill-conditioned ridge solve
reaching the dynamics fit is a correctness concern, not cosmetic noise.

\textbf{Dataset revisions are not pinned.} A Hub-side update will change these numbers.

\section{Next Steps}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item Test the ill-conditioning hypothesis: log the condition number of the design matrix
per dataset and check whether it predicts the \textsc{high} findings. If it does, the fix is
regularization or abstention, not a new threshold.
\item Make the residual threshold control-rate aware, or verify that it already is.
\item Publish a precision estimate with an explicit $N$ from hand-adjudicated findings.
\item Run a policy-outcome experiment on at least one detector, converting a mechanism
argument into a measurement.
\item Resolve the embodiment-metadata gap before shipping a default calibration corpus.
\end{enumerate}
